Tanda mainnet

The savings circle nobody can run off with.

A tanda (also called susu, chit fund, tontine or ROSCA) is how hundreds of millions of people save: everyone chips in every round and one member takes the whole pot, in turns. Its weak spot has always been trust. Here a Bitcoin Cash covenant holds the pot and can only pay it to the member whose turn it is, so not even the organizer can take it.

How it works

1

Create

The organizer lists the members in payout order and the amount per round. One transaction deploys the rules and mints a small NFT that tracks the round.

2

Pay in

Every round, each member sends their share to the contract address from any BCH wallet. Everyone sees the pot fill up on the chain.

3

Release

When the pot is full, anyone presses Release. The covenant only lets it go to the member whose turn it is, and moves the round forward.

What the contract guarantees

  • The pot can only be paid to the member whose turn it is, never to anyone else, the organizer included.
  • It can only be paid when it is complete: members × amount.
  • The round counter (an NFT inside the contract) moves forward exactly one step per payout and can't leave the contract.
  • Money paid ahead for later rounds stays in the contract. After the last round, everything left goes to the last member.
  • Nobody can burn the pot in fees: a payout may spend at most a small fixed fee.
  • No keys are needed to release a pot, so a tanda keeps working even if the organizer disappears.

What it can't do: force a member to keep paying after they've had their turn. That part of a tanda is still social, as it always was. The difference is that everyone can see on the chain who has paid.
